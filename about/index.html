About FilmyChill

FilmyChill answers one question: what's actually worth watching this week? Not a database of everything ever made — a short, honest, daily-refreshed list of this week's theatre and streaming releases in India, US, UK, Australia, Germany, UAE, Canada, Singapore, Malaysia, Philippines, New Zealand, South Korea, Japan & Indonesia, each with a rating, a plain-language verdict and a one-line critics' take.

How the picks are chosen

The lists are rebuilt automatically twice a day. Titles qualify only if they're genuinely current — new theatrical releases, new seasons, or titles that just arrived on a streaming platform (we track first sightings ourselves, so a 2024 film that lands on Netflix today correctly counts as news today). Within that fresh pool, ranking blends audience rating, vote volume and recency, with a quality floor for the Pick of the Week. Older titles that are still worth your time appear below a clearly marked divider — they never impersonate this week's news.

When we don't know yet, we say so. A title in its first week with only a handful of votes gets no rating, no verdict and no critics' take — the card says "just released, verdict soon" and stays that way until real numbers arrive. That gate covers the critics' line too, not just the score: a settled critical consensus does not exist three days after release, and printing one next to "verdict soon" would contradict itself. A rating that has been sitting on nine votes for months is a different case — thin turnout from viewers, not an unsettled verdict from critics — so those titles keep their line.

The FilmyChill Score

Every film gets a FilmyChill Score: our verdict, worked out automatically from two signals and refreshed twice a day. The audience signal is the film's rating on TMDB, and it only counts once 50 or more people have rated it: 7.5 and up is loved, 6.5 to 7.4 is liked, below that is lukewarm. The critics signal is the overall reception in published reviews, as summarised in the film's Wikipedia article: positive, mixed or negative.

When audiences and critics both love a film, it's a Must watch. When one side is keen and the other is split or cooler, it's Worth a watch, and the card says which side is which. It's a Skip only when audiences are lukewarm and critics aren't won over either. With only one signal, the score uses that one and says so. With neither, it says so plainly — too early for something just released, not enough ratings with the real count for anything older — rather than guess.

Films with few ratings. A film that has been out for two months or more may never reach 50 ratings, so from 15 it gets a score anyway, built to be careful: the rating is weighted toward an average film (the fewer the ratings, the stronger the pull, so a handful of fans or critics can't swing it), it can't be a Must watch, and it is always labelled with its count — for example “Skip · Based on 21 ratings”. We only say “too early” about films that have just come out; for anything older we say how many ratings there are. The audience rating is always shown next to the score, so you can see how we got there.

Where the data comes from

Film metadata, ratings and streaming availability come from TMDB (this site uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB; streaming data via JustWatch). Trending signals come from Wikipedia readership and YouTube trailer statistics. The "Critics' take" line on each film is synthesised in our own words from the critical-reception coverage of that film's Wikipedia article — a human-written summary of real published reviews — or, when that doesn't exist yet, from early viewer review averages on TMDB. When there isn't enough signal to say something honest, we say nothing.

Your votes

Film pages ask “Watched it? Was it worth it?”. If you tap an answer, your browser creates an anonymous ID (no name, email or sign-up) and saves one small record: that ID, the film, your answer, which FilmyChill country page you were on, and the time. Records are stored with Google Firebase and used only to count how many people found each film worth it. One vote per film per browser, and tapping again changes it. Your answer is also remembered in your browser (local storage, not a cookie) so the page shows what you chose; nothing is saved at all unless you tap, and the ID is never used to follow what else you read. Votes are collected quietly for now; once films have enough of them, they will become part of the FilmyChill Score.

What we don't do

No pay-for-placement — no studio, platform or distributor can buy a position on any list, ever. No fake urgency: dates on the site are real dates, and archived pages say they're archived. No tracking, either: FilmyChill sets no cookies, has no account to create, and never profiles you. The site does count page views, using GoatCounter — a cookieless counter that records the page, the referrer and nothing that identifies a visitor, and that respects Do Not Track. It exists so we can tell which pages are worth keeping, and it is the only third-party script on the site. Your My List saves to your own browser's storage and is never sent anywhere — which is also why it doesn't follow you to another device. If FilmyChill ever carries advertising or affiliate links, they'll be clearly separated from the editorial rankings, which will keep working exactly as described above.
